The pool pump is usually the second largest electricity consumer in a house with a pool, behind the air conditioner. Fall is the right time to pull back on run time. It is also the easiest time to overdo it.

## What run time is actually for

The goal is turnover: moving the full volume of the pool through the filter. Most pools want roughly one full turnover per day, though that is a guideline rather than a law.

In summer, higher water temperature and heavier bather load mean you want more. In cooler water with nobody swimming, you genuinely need less.

## A reasonable fall adjustment

If you ran ten or twelve hours in August, something in the six to eight hour range is often appropriate for late October, and less again in deep winter.

Make the change gradually and watch the water. If it starts looking slightly dull or the chlorine gets harder to hold, you have cut too far. Add an hour back.

## The leaf season exception

Here is where people get burned. Just as you are cutting run time for the season, leaf drop peaks.

Heavy debris load actually argues for _more_ circulation, not less, because you need the skimmers working and the filter capturing material. Cutting to minimum run time in the middle of November leaf drop is one of the more reliable ways to end up with a cloudy pool in December.

Our suggestion: make the big cut after the leaves finish, not before they start.

## If you have a variable-speed pump

Variable-speed pumps save substantial money, but only if they are set sensibly. Running one at full speed for six hours largely wastes the advantage.

Lower speeds move less water per hour but use dramatically less electricity, so a longer run at a lower speed usually costs less and filters better than a short run at full speed. The efficiency gain is not linear, and that is the whole point of the pump.

If yours was installed and never configured past the installer's defaults, it is worth having someone look at the schedule. We check this on service visits and tell you when the settings are working against the water.

## Do not go to zero

Whatever you do, the pump should run every day, year round. A pool that sits with no circulation goes bad quickly, even in cold water, and stagnant water in winter also removes your freeze protection.
