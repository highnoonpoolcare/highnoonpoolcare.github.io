Most North Texas pool owners stop swimming several weeks before they actually have to. October afternoons here are frequently in the eighties, and the water is often warmer than people assume.

## Water lags air, and that works in your favor

A pool holds heat. Water temperature trails air temperature by a week or two, which means the water in early October is still carrying a good deal of September.

Unheated pools around here typically stay swimmable into the middle of October, sometimes later. The thing that ends it is usually not a gradual cooling but the first real cold front, which can pull water temperature down several degrees in a couple of days.

## What actually drives the cooling

**Overnight lows** matter far more than daytime highs. A week of eighty-five degree afternoons with fifty degree nights will cool a pool steadily, because pools lose most of their heat overnight through the surface.

**Wind** accelerates it considerably. Pools in Lake Dallas and along the western edge of Lewisville, where wind comes off Lake Lewisville, cool noticeably faster than sheltered pools a few miles inland.

**Surface area** works against large pools and any pool with an attached spa or water feature running.

## If you want more season

A **solar cover** is the cheapest meaningful step. It cuts overnight heat loss substantially and costs nothing to run. It is also a nuisance to handle, which is why most people who buy one stop using it by November.

A **heater** genuinely extends the season, and in North Texas it can make a pool usable most of the year. Running one is not cheap, but the milder climate here means it works far less hard than it would further north.

If you have an attached spa, heating just the spa through the winter is often the better value. It is a small volume of water and it gets used on exactly the cold evenings when the pool does not.

## Either way, the pool keeps running

Whether you swim in October or not, the pool still needs circulation and a baseline of chlorine. Fall is not the off season here, it is just the quieter part of it.
