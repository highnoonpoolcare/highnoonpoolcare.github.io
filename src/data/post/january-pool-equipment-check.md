Most pool equipment failures announce themselves well before they happen. The trouble is that nobody is standing at the equipment pad in January listening for them.

Here is a walkthrough worth doing once, now, while the season is quiet.

## Look for freeze damage you may have missed

If we have already had a hard freeze this winter, inspect the pad carefully.

Look for hairline cracks on the pump housing and filter tank, dampness or mineral trails around fittings, and any weeping at seals. A crack that has not fully failed will often show as a small wet patch or a white deposit where water has evaporated.

Finding this in January means a planned repair. Finding it in June means an emergency.

## Listen to the pump

Start the pump and listen. Grinding, screeching, or a rising whine all indicate bearing wear, and bearings do not recover.

A pump that sounds worse than it did last spring is telling you something. Motors generally give months of warning if anyone is listening.

## Check your filter pressure baseline

With a genuinely clean filter, note the pressure. Write it on the tank with a marker.

That number is the reference for the whole year. Without it, you are guessing about when the filter needs cleaning, and guessing usually means cleaning too late.

## Inspect the cartridge or media

Pull the cartridge and look at the pleats. Cartridges do not last forever; pleats break down, seams split, and a tired cartridge filters poorly no matter how well you clean it.

For sand filters, consider whether the media is due. Sand is typically good for several years, after which it channels and hardens, and no amount of backwashing helps.

## Check the salt cell, if you have one

Salt cells scale up, particularly in hard North Texas water. Inspect the plates and clean them if deposits have formed.

Cells also have a finite life measured in operating hours. If yours is several years old and your chlorine production has been dropping, that is likely why.

## Look at the valves and unions

Turn each valve. Stiff or seized valves are easier to deal with now than the day you urgently need to redirect flow.

Check union fittings for cracks, especially if they have been through a freeze.

## Write down what you find

Keep a note of what you checked and when. When something does eventually fail, knowing what it sounded like six months earlier makes the diagnosis much faster.
