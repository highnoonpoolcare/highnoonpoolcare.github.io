A lot of people move to Denton County from places with real winters, and they arrive with a mental model of pool ownership that includes closing the pool in October and opening it in May.

That model does not fit here, and following it causes problems.

## What closing actually means

In a northern climate, winterizing means lowering the water below the skimmer, blowing the lines clear, adding antifreeze to the plumbing, and covering the pool solid for months. It exists because the ground freezes and water in the plumbing would freeze solid and split it.

Our ground does not freeze that way. We get short, sharp freezes rather than sustained ones.

## Why closing here backfires

**Stagnant water goes bad.** A pool sitting unfiltered for four months, even cold, develops problems. Algae is slower in cold water, not absent. Spring opening on a closed North Texas pool is frequently a green-to-clean job.

**You lose your freeze protection.** The best defense against our kind of freeze is moving water. A closed system with the pump off has none.

**Our winters are interrupted.** We get seventy degree days in January. A closed pool cannot take advantage of them, and the temperature swings are harder on a static pool than a circulating one.

## What we do instead

**Keep circulating, less.** The pump runs every day, just fewer hours. Circulation continues, turnover continues, freeze protection stays available.

**Hold a lower chlorine baseline.** Cold water needs less sanitizer, but it needs some. Letting chlorine hit zero for weeks is how winter algae starts.

**Keep testing, less often.** Chemistry moves slowly in cold water. Checking every week or two is usually enough, versus the closer attention summer demands.

**Keep debris out.** This matters more than people expect, because organic material breaking down over months is what makes spring difficult.

## The spring payoff

The real argument for staying open is what happens in March. A pool that circulated all winter needs a chemistry adjustment and a good clean, and it is ready.

A pool that sat closed often needs a full recovery, which costs considerably more than the winter electricity you saved.
