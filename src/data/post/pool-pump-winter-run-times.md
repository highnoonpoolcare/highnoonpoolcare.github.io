Winter is when the pool actually saves you money, and pump run time is where most of that saving comes from. There are two rules, and the second one overrides the first completely.

## Rule one: less is genuinely fine

Cold water, no swimmers, weak sun, slow algae. You need much less turnover than in summer.

Many North Texas pools do perfectly well on four to six hours a day through the coldest stretch. That is a substantial reduction from summer hours, and it shows up on the bill.

Watch the water rather than the clock. If it stays clear and chlorine holds steadily between tests, your run time is sufficient. If the water dulls or chlorine keeps dropping, add hours back.

## Rule two: during a freeze, run it continuously

This overrides everything above.

When temperatures approach freezing, the pump should run continuously for the duration, regardless of your winter schedule. Moving water is the primary defense against equipment damage, and equipment damage costs vastly more than a few days of electricity.

If you have an automatic freeze guard, it should handle this. Verify that it works before you rely on it, because a disabled freeze guard is something we find regularly.

If you do not have one, override your timer manually when a front is coming. Set a phone reminder when you see the forecast.

## Run during the day, not overnight

In summer, overnight running is often recommended to take advantage of cooler temperatures and off-peak rates.

In winter, flip that. Running during the warmer daylight hours is safer, because it means the system is less likely to be sitting still during the coldest part of the night. If a surprise cold snap arrives, you are better positioned.

## Variable-speed pumps in winter

If you have one, winter is where it shines. A long run at a low speed uses remarkably little electricity while maintaining continuous circulation, which is close to ideal for our climate.

For freeze protection specifically, continuous low-speed running is often better than a shorter high-speed run, because what matters is that water keeps moving.

## Never go to zero

Whatever schedule you land on, the pump should run every single day. A pool with no circulation develops problems even in cold water, and a system sitting completely still has no freeze protection at all.
