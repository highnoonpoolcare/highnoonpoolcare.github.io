If you live under mature pecans or post oaks, you already know what is coming. Leaf drop in Denton County is not a gentle scattering. It arrives in waves, and a pool can go from clear to covered in a single windy afternoon.

## Why leaves are worse than they look

A floating leaf is a minor annoyance. A sunk leaf is a problem.

Organic material on the pool floor breaks down and consumes chlorine, which is why pools test persistently low in November no matter how much sanitizer goes in. Tannins from decaying leaves also stain plaster, and those stains can be genuinely difficult to remove once set.

The window matters. A leaf skimmed off the surface today costs you nothing. The same leaf vacuumed off the floor next week may have already left a mark.

## Skim first, everything else second

During peak drop, surface skimming is the highest-value thing you can do, and it beats any chemical adjustment. Get material out before it sinks.

If you are handling the pool yourself, a few minutes with a net every day or two through November will do more than a weekend deep clean.

## Baskets, constantly

Skimmer and pump baskets fill fast this time of year. In Highland Village we see baskets go from empty to packed within a few days.

A packed basket means restricted flow, which means a struggling pump and poor circulation exactly when you need turnover most.

## Consider a cover, with a caveat

A mesh leaf cover during peak drop saves an enormous amount of work, particularly if you are not swimming much in November anyway.

The caveat: a covered pool still needs circulation and chemistry. Covering a pool and walking away for six weeks is how people end up calling us in December about water that has gone swamp green underneath.

## When a second visit makes sense

Some of our customers in the heaviest tree cover add a second weekly visit from roughly early November through mid-December. It is not something we recommend by default, and for most pools it is unnecessary.

If your pool sits directly under mature trees, though, the math often works out in favor of the extra visit rather than fighting a losing battle for six weeks.
