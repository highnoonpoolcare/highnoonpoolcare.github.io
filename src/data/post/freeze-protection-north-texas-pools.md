This is the most important thing we write about all year. North Texas gets relatively few hard freezes, and precisely because they are uncommon, a lot of pools are unprepared when one arrives.

The damage is not subtle. Cracked pump housings, split filters, burst plumbing, ruined heaters. February 2021 taught this region an expensive lesson.

## Moving water is the whole strategy

Water that is circulating does not freeze at the same rate as water sitting still. The single most effective thing you can do during a freeze is keep the pump running continuously.

Not on a timer. Not overnight only. Continuously, for the duration of the freeze event.

If your system has an automatic freeze guard, confirm it is working before you need it. If it does not, run the pump manually and leave it running.

## What to do before the front arrives

**Clean the filter.** A restricted filter reduces flow, and reduced flow is exactly what you do not want when you are relying on circulation.

**Clear the baskets.** Same reason.

**Check water level.** If water drops below the skimmer, the pump loses prime and stops moving water, which defeats the entire plan. This is a common failure mode.

**Run any water features.** Waterfalls and sheer descents hold water in exposed plumbing. Either run them or drain them.

## If the power goes out

This is the genuinely dangerous scenario, and it is what caused most of the damage in 2021.

With no power, the pump stops and water sits in the equipment. If the outage will be long and temperatures are severe, the safer move is to drain the pump, filter, and heater rather than hope.

Most pumps and filters have drain plugs. Open them, let the water out, and leave them open until the freeze passes. A dry pump survives a freeze. A full one often does not.

Label where those plugs are now, while it is comfortable outside.

## Afterward

Once temperatures recover, close the drain plugs, prime the pump, and start the system before you assume everything is fine. Check for weeping at fittings and seals, which is often the first sign of a crack that has not fully failed yet.

Then check the water chemistry, which will have drifted while you were reasonably distracted by other things.
