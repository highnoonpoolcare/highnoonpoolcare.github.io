Winter chemistry is genuinely easier than summer chemistry. Cold water holds chlorine better, algae grows slower, and bather load drops to nothing. That is real, and you should enjoy it.

It is not, however, an excuse to stop entirely.

## Chlorine: lower, never zero

Chlorine demand in fifty degree water is a fraction of what it is in ninety degree water. You will use dramatically less.

The failure mode is letting it reach zero and stay there for weeks. Algae growth slows in cold water but does not stop, and a pool that sits with no sanitizer through December and January will show you exactly what that looks like in February.

Hold a modest baseline. Check every week or two rather than every few days.

## pH still drifts

pH does not take the winter off. It tends to rise in most pools, and high pH makes whatever chlorine you have less effective, which compounds the problem.

This is the one winter number genuinely worth staying on top of.

## Alkalinity and hardness: check monthly

Total alkalinity is your pH buffer, and if it drifts out of range, pH becomes unstable and difficult to hold.

Calcium hardness is worth a monthly check, and winter is the right season to address it if it climbed over the summer. A partial drain and refill in January will not be immediately undone by evaporation the way it would in July.

## Cold water changes how corrections behave

Chemical reactions slow down considerably in cold water. Adjustments take longer to show up on a test, and the natural temptation is to add more because nothing seems to be happening.

Resist that. Make an adjustment, give it a full circulation cycle or more, then retest. Overdosing a cold pool is easy and correcting it is tedious.

## Stabilizer: leave it alone

Cyanuric acid protects chlorine from sunlight. Winter sun is weaker and days are shorter, so you need less of it.

More to the point, it does not degrade meaningfully, so whatever you had in October is still there. Adding more in winter accomplishes nothing except pushing you toward the levels that cause trouble next summer.

## A realistic winter rhythm

Test every week or two. Adjust pH as needed, keep a modest chlorine baseline, check alkalinity and hardness monthly, keep debris out, and keep the pump running daily.

That is genuinely the whole job until March.
