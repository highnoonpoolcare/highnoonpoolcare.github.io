Holiday guests are not going to swim. They are going to look at it, though, usually through the back windows while standing around the kitchen, and a neglected pool is conspicuous.

The good news is that a winter pool cleans up quickly because there is no algae bloom racing you.

## Skim and net the floor

Surface debris and leaves on the floor are ninety percent of what makes a winter pool look bad. Twenty minutes with a net does more for appearance than anything chemical.

If leaves have been sitting a while, check for staining after you remove them. Tannin stains on plaster are easier to address early.

## Brush the waterline

The waterline is what people actually see from a distance. A quick pass with a brush removes the film that accumulates and makes an enormous visual difference.

If you have hard calcium scale rather than just grime, that will not brush off and needs a proper treatment. Worth knowing the difference before you scrub at it for an hour.

## Clear the baskets and check the water level

Practical rather than cosmetic, but December is freeze season and both of these matter for freeze protection. A pool below the skimmer line cannot circulate properly, and circulation is your defense on a cold night.

## Run the pump a bit longer for a few days

Cold water is slow to respond, so give yourself lead time. Running a couple of extra hours for two or three days beforehand helps clear any haze and gets the water looking its best.

Do not wait until the morning guests arrive to notice the water looks dull. In January it may take three days to fix what takes a few hours in July.

## Check the lights

If your pool has lights and you have not turned them on since September, test them now. A lit pool looks genuinely good on a winter evening, and a burned-out bulb is easier to deal with before company arrives than during.

## Then leave it alone

Once it looks right, winter chemistry holds well. There is no need to fuss at it daily the way you might in August.

Skim, brush, check the level, and enjoy having people over.
