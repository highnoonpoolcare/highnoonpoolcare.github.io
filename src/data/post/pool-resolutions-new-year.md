Winter is the cheapest and least disruptive time to deal with pool problems. Nobody is swimming, contractors are less booked than they will be in May, and a few days of downtime costs you nothing.

Here are the five things most worth handling now.

## 1. The equipment you know is on borrowed time

If your pump has been noisy since August, or the filter has needed replacing for two seasons, deal with it now.

Equipment fails in July. That is not really a coincidence: heat, long run times, and heavy load are exactly what push marginal equipment over the edge. Replacing on your schedule in January is considerably less painful than replacing on its schedule during a heat wave.

## 2. Calcium hardness that climbed all summer

If hardness crept up over the season, winter is when to address it with a partial drain and refill.

Do it in July and evaporation immediately starts concentrating it again. Do it in January and the correction holds for months.

## 3. Stabilizer that has accumulated

Cyanuric acid builds up over a season and does not leave on its own. If yours has climbed too high, your chlorine will underperform all next summer no matter what you do.

The fix is dilution, and it pairs naturally with the hardness drain above.

## 4. The freeze protection you have never tested

If you have an automatic freeze guard and have never verified it works, do that before the coldest part of winter rather than after.

We regularly find freeze guards that have been disabled or have failed sensors, and the owner has no idea. It is a five minute check that prevents a four figure repair.

## 5. Whatever surface problem you have been ignoring

Stains, scale at the waterline, a rough patch of plaster, a cracked tile. These do not improve on their own, and they are much easier to address in an empty season.

Surface work in winter also means the pool is genuinely ready in April rather than being worked on during the first warm weekend.

## The honest one

If last summer was a constant fight and the pool was never quite right, the problem is usually consistency rather than knowledge.

Pools do not need heroic effort. They need someone paying attention every week. That is worth thinking about before the season starts rather than in the middle of it.
