We have written about how to protect a pool during a freeze. This one is about why those steps work, because understanding the failure modes makes the advice much easier to take seriously.

## Water expands, and your equipment does not

Water expands by roughly nine percent when it freezes. Inside a sealed pump housing, filter tank, or length of pipe, that expansion has nowhere to go.

Something has to give, and it is always the housing.

## The pump

The pump is the most common casualty. The volute, the housing that holds water around the impeller, cracks when water inside it freezes.

Pump housings are not designed to flex. A crack is usually not repairable, and you are replacing the wet end or the whole pump.

The failure is also frequently not obvious immediately. A hairline crack may hold until you restart the system, then weep or spray.

## The filter

Filter tanks hold a substantial volume of standing water. Cartridge and DE tanks can split at the seam or crack in the body.

Sand filters have an additional problem: the multiport valve on top contains small passages that are easy to crack and expensive to replace.

## The heater

Heaters are the costliest thing to lose. The heat exchanger is a dense assembly of small tubes, and small tubes with water in them are exactly what freezes first.

A cracked heat exchanger is frequently a heater replacement rather than a repair.

## Plumbing and valves

Exposed PVC above ground is vulnerable, and so are the valves on the pad. Water features and sheer descents are particularly exposed because their plumbing often sits high and holds water when not running.

Below-ground plumbing is generally safer here, since our ground does not freeze deeply, which is exactly why the equipment pad is where the damage concentrates.

## Why circulation works

Moving water resists freezing considerably better than still water. As long as the pump is running and water is flowing through the whole system, the risk drops enormously.

This is why the advice is always to run the pump continuously through a freeze rather than on a schedule.

## Why draining works when power fails

If the pump cannot run, circulation is not available, and the only remaining strategy is removing the water that would expand.

Open the drain plugs on the pump and filter, drain the heater if it has a drain, and leave them open. Equipment with no water inside survives a freeze indefinitely.
