# Blog image shot list

Right now all 16 posts share `hero_pool.png` — one clean pool, regardless of whether
the article is about a cracked pump housing or calcium scale on tile. Each post below
has the shot it actually needs.

**Workflow:** photograph these on your routes → save as
`src/assets/images/blog/<slug>.jpg` → run `node scripts/link-blog-images.mjs`.
Posts without a photo keep the default hero, so you can do these a few at a time.

**Specs:** landscape, roughly 16:9, 1600x900 or larger, uncompressed JPG. Astro resizes
and converts to WebP at build.

> Most of these are things you already see every week. A phone camera in good light is
> genuinely fine — originality matters more than production value here, and the same
> shots are reusable on your Google Business Profile.

## Fall

### 1. `fall-pool-prep-north-texas` — 2026-09-24

**Getting Your Pool Through a North Texas Fall**

Clean pool with the first scattering of leaves on the surface, late-afternoon light.
Should read as "season turning," not "neglected." A skimmer in frame helps.

### 2. `pool-filter-october-north-texas` — 2026-10-01

**We Clean More Filters in October Than in July**

Close-up of a **filter pressure gauge with the needle readable**. This is the number
the whole article is about. A dirty pulled cartridge standing beside a clean one is an
excellent alternative — the contrast tells the story instantly.

### 3. `leaf-drop-season-pool-care` — 2026-10-08

**When the Pecans Start Dropping, Skim First**

Heavy leaf load: a **packed skimmer basket** pulled and held, or a pool surface covered
after a windy afternoon under mature trees. This is the one place a messy pool is the
right image.

### 4. `swimming-season-water-temperature` — 2026-10-15

**Most People Quit Swimming Three Weeks Too Early**

Inviting pool in autumn light — bare or turning trees in the background, water still
clearly swimmable. A floating thermometer showing a usable temperature would be ideal.

### 5. `calcium-hardness-north-texas` — 2026-10-22

**Hard Water Is What Kills Heaters Around Here**

**Calcium scale**: the chalky white line on waterline tile, or scaled-up salt cell
plates. A scaled cell next to a clean one is the strongest version of this shot.

### 6. `pump-run-time-fall` — 2026-10-29

**Cutting Pump Hours Without Turning the Pool Green**

**Variable-speed pump control panel** showing the schedule or RPM display. A standard
timer with the dial pins visible works too. Equipment, not water.

## Early winter

### 7. `first-cold-front-pool-checklist` — 2026-11-05

**The First Norther Is Your Cue**

Equipment pad under a grey front rolling in, or a hand at the equipment breaker.
Should feel like "weather is coming, go check things."

### 8. `freeze-protection-north-texas-pools` — 2026-11-12

**Keep the Pump Running When It Freezes**

**Water visibly moving** in a cold pool — a return jet rippling the surface with frost
on the decking or coping. The whole point of the article is that circulation is the
defense, so motion needs to be visible.

### 9. `why-we-dont-close-pools-north-texas` — 2026-11-19

**We Don't Close Pools Down Here**

Clear, open, circulating pool in obvious winter conditions — bare trees, winter light,
no cover. The image _is_ the argument: pools stay open here.

### 10. `winter-pool-chemistry` — 2026-11-26

**Winter Chemistry Is Easier, Not Optional**

**Test kit in use** — comparator block or filled test vials, ideally in a gloved or
cold-weather hand. Shows testing continuing through winter.

## Deep winter

### 11. `hard-freeze-pool-equipment-damage` — 2026-12-03

**What Cracks First in a Hard Freeze**

**A cracked pump housing or split filter tank.** You have almost certainly photographed
one of these already. If not, keep the next failed wet end you pull — this is the single
most valuable image on the list, because nobody else has it.

### 12. `winter-algae-north-texas` — 2026-12-10

**Algae Doesn't Take the Winter Off**

**Early-stage algae**: faint green shadowing on steps or in a corner where circulation
is weakest. Not a full green pool — the article is specifically about catching it early.

### 13. `holiday-pool-prep` — 2026-12-17

**Nobody's Swimming, But Everyone's Looking at It**

Clean pool at dusk with the pool light on, ideally **framed from inside through a window
or door**. The article's premise is that the pool is the view from the kitchen.

### 14. `pool-pump-winter-run-times` — 2026-12-24

**How Few Hours Can You Run the Pump in January?**

Pump timer or VSD display showing a **short winter schedule**. Distinguish from #6 by
shooting the schedule/hours rather than the pump itself.

## New year

### 15. `pool-resolutions-new-year` — 2026-12-31

**Five Things to Fix Before Next Summer**

**Aging equipment**: a tired pad, corroded fittings, a pump that has clearly done its
time. This one should look like a to-do list, in contrast to #16.

### 16. `january-pool-equipment-check` — 2027-01-07

**Thirty Minutes at the Equipment Pad in January**

**A well-maintained equipment pad, running.** Clean pump and filter, tidy plumbing,
pressure gauge readable. This is the "here is what good looks like" shot and the direct
counterpoint to #15 — same subject, opposite condition.

Explicitly **not** a photo of clean water. The article never mentions the pool itself.

---

## Reuse notes

- #6 and #14 are both pump controls. Shoot them on different days or different equipment
  so they do not look like the same photo twice.
- #15 and #16 are deliberately paired — neglected pad vs maintained pad. Shooting them
  the same afternoon on two different properties makes the contrast sharper.
- #11 (cracked housing) is the hardest to stage and the most valuable. Grab it
  opportunistically whenever a freeze failure comes through.
- Every one of these doubles as Google Business Profile content, which is separately the
  highest-impact local SEO work available to you.
